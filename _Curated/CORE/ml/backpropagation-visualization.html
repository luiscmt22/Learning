Backpropagation Visualized
An interactive SVG-based visualization of a tiny neural network (6 inputs → 6 hidden → 8 outputs) walking through the four stages of backpropagation: forward pass, output errors, error propagation backward, and weight update.
Neural networks don't learn by magic — they learn by getting things wrong and then distributing the blame backward through every weight that contributed to the wrong answer. Most explanations of backprop are a wall of chain-rule calculus. Here's one with circles, arrows, and a button labeled '1. Forward Pass' — press it and watch the math light up.
Executive summary
This is a self-contained interactive HTML page that visualizes how a feedforward neural network classifies animals from 6 binary-ish inputs (Fly? Swim? Mammal? Bird? Feline? Danger?) and learns from mistakes via backpropagation. The user clicks four buttons to step through: (1) forward pass — data flows left-to-right and each neuron computes sigmoid(Σ weighted inputs); (2) output errors — compare predictions to expected and color-code the gap; (3) backpropagation — highlight the error flowing backward through the hidden layer; (4) weight update — show which weights get nudged. It's intentionally pedagogical, not a library — the aim is intuition, not accuracy.
💡
Metaphor
Imagine a classroom where 6 students whisper answers to 6 teachers in the middle row, who then whisper to 8 judges at the back. The judges raise paddles with their guess. If the judges are wrong, the 'correction' whisper runs in reverse — each judge tells the middle teachers 'you nudged me the wrong way,' and the middle teachers tell the students 'you fed me bad info.' The next round, everyone whispers slightly differently. That's backpropagation — a chain of 'who's partially to blame?' running backward through the network.
