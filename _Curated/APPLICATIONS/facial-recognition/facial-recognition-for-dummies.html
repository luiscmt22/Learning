Facial Recognition - For Dummies

A plain-language explainer of the facial recognition attendance system that shows how a face becomes 128 numbers, how those numbers are matched against a 'phonebook' of known faces, and why only the numbers — never the photo — leave the device.

MD Beginner 540 words 3 min read Source: Education/ForDummies/07-Facial-Recognition-Architecture-ForDummies.md

When you look at a kiosk camera and it says 'Welcome, João' two seconds later, you might think your photo is sitting on a server somewhere. It isn't. What's actually on the server is 128 little numbers — and those numbers can't be turned back into a face, no matter how hard anyone tries.

Executive summary

This is the non-technical companion to the HRModule facial recognition architecture doc. It explains the same system in four friendly steps: phone sees your face, ML turns it into 128 numbers, server compares those numbers against the 'face phonebook,' and if there's a match it welcomes you. It also covers why 10 consecutive detections are required (so a photo doesn't work) and the privacy story (numbers can't be reversed into a face — like knowing someone's height and weight doesn't let you draw them).

💡
MetaphorYour face has 128 secret measurements — the distance between your eyes, how wide your jaw is, where your mouth sits. The camera reads those measurements and scribbles them on a slip of paper. The slip goes to the server, which has a filing cabinet of everyone's slips. It flips through until it finds one that matches yours. And the slip? You couldn't draw a face from it any more than you could draw me from my height and weight.

1Why it exists

Because the engineering architecture doc is dense and uses words like 'JSInvokable,' 'Euclidean distance,' and 'descriptor' — which is fine for developers but useless for anyone who just wants to understand how the login kiosk knows who they are. This version exists so a manager, a new hire, or a curious employee can grasp the system in five minutes without touching any code.

2What it does

Walks the reader through the four-step flow of a facial check-in — camera sees face, math turns face into 128 numbers, server compares numbers to stored ones, welcome message — and answers the two most common questions non-technical people have: 'is someone storing my photo?' and 'can I fool it with a picture?'

3How it works

It leans on analogies: face-to-numbers is like a fingerprint, server comparison is like a phonebook lookup, the 10-detection requirement is like needing to confirm a password twice, and the privacy explanation is 'knowing someone's height and weight doesn't let you draw them.' The visual flow is shown with ASCII art of a camera, a face emoji, a progress bar, and a welcome screen. There is no code — just concepts, emojis, and simple diagrams.

  • server-side 'phonebook' matching
  • 10-detection anti-spoofing

How does the app know it's YOU?

The Simple Explanation

When you look at a camera and the app says "Welcome, João!", how does it know?

Step 1: Your Phone Sees Your Face

The camera on your phone/tablet captures your face.

     📷
      │
      │  "I see a face!"
      ▼
   ┌─────────┐
   │  😀     │
   │  Your   │
   │  Face   │
   └─────────┘

Step 2: Math Turns Your Face Into Numbers

A special program (face-api.js) looks at your face and measures things like:

  • Distance between your eyes
  • Shape of your nose
  • Width of your jaw
  • Position of your mouth
  • ...and 124 more measurements!
   😀 Your Face
      │
      │  "Let me measure this..."
      ▼
   [0.12, -0.45, 0.78, 0.23, -0.11, ...]

   (128 numbers that describe YOUR unique face)

Think of it like a fingerprint, but with numbers!

Step 3: The Server Compares

The server has a "phonebook" of faces:

   📖 Face Phonebook:

   João Silva:   [0.12, -0.45, 0.78, ...]
   Maria Santos: [0.34, 0.12, -0.56, ...]
   Pedro Costa:  [-0.23, 0.89, 0.11, ...]

When your numbers arrive, the server checks:

   Your numbers: [0.12, -0.45, 0.78, ...]

   João's numbers: [0.12, -0.45, 0.78, ...]

   ✓ Almost identical! You must be João!

Step 4: Welcome!

   ┌─────────────────────────┐
   │      Welcome!           │
   │                         │
   │   👤 João Silva         │
   │                         │
   │   [Check-In] [Break]    │
   └─────────────────────────┘

Why Does It Need 10 Tries?

The camera checks your face 10 times in a row to make sure:

   Try 1:  😀 → [numbers] ✓ Looks like João
   Try 2:  😀 → [numbers] ✓ Still looks like João
   Try 3:  😀 → [numbers] ✓ Yep, João
   ...
   Try 10: 😀 → [numbers] ✓ Definitely João!

This prevents mistakes and makes sure someone isn't holding up a photo!

Why Is It Secure?

What STAYS on Your Phone:

  • Your actual face image 📷
  • The camera video 🎥

What GOES to the Server:

  • Just the 128 numbers 🔢
  • (No one can rebuild your face from numbers!)
   ❌ Photo never sent
   ✅ Only math numbers sent

It's like sending your height and weight instead of a photo - no one can draw your face from "180cm, 75kg"!

Key Takeaways

  1. Your face → Numbers (128 measurements)
  2. Numbers sent to server (not your photo!)
  3. Server compares numbers (like matching fingerprints)
  4. 10 checks for accuracy (no photos allowed!)
  5. Privacy protected (can't rebuild face from numbers)

For the technical details, see 07-Facial-Recognition-Architecture.md

Key takeaways

  • Your face becomes 128 numerical measurements — like a mathematical fingerprint
  • Only the numbers are sent to the server, never your actual photo
  • The server compares your numbers to the stored numbers of known employees
  • 10 consecutive checks are required so a held-up photo can't fool the system
  • Numbers cannot be reversed into a face, so they're safe to transmit
