SignalR Notification System For Dummies

Beginner walkthrough of the multi-target notification system: how to address messages by Group, Department, Job/Project or Role, how SignalR groups auto-join on connect, why offline users still get the message, and what the EnvioGlobal sender UI looks like.

MD Beginner 1,127 words 6 min read Source: Education/ForDummies/14-SignalR-Notification-System-ForDummies.md

How does 'send to IT Department' actually become 'Maria's phone buzzes'? The trip from a label on a tab to a buzzing notification involves two databases, a deduper, and the world's politest voicemail.

Executive summary

Part 2 of the SignalR ForDummies pair. Introduces the notification system as a 'shopping mall PA system' with multiple targeting tabs (Grupos, Departamentos, Obras, Funcoes), explains the address-resolution chain (target -> employees -> users), walks through how every connected user is auto-joined to several SignalR groups (User_*, All, Company_*, Dept_*, Job_*, Role_*), explains the offline 'voicemail' fallback where notifications are persisted in the database for later, and walks through the four-step EnvioGlobal sender UI. Designed for someone who has never used SignalR before.

💡
MetaphorThe notification system is the mall PA system. Every shopper walks in carrying a walkie-talkie tuned to several channels at once: their personal channel, the all-shoppers channel, their store's channel, and any event channels they signed up for. When the manager wants to call the IT department to a meeting, she does not need every shopper's name - she just keys the IT channel and only the IT walkie-talkies crackle. And if your walkie-talkie is off, the message lands in your locker so you find it on your way out.

1Why it exists

The team needed to push notifications to many different audiences - not just user groups, but everyone in a department, everyone working on a project, everyone with a particular job title - and they needed both Blazor pages and external systems (mobile, integrations, background jobs) to be able to fire those notifications. They also could not afford to lose messages just because the recipient was offline at the moment of sending.

2What it does

Lets a sender pick a target type (Grupo, Departamento, Obra, Funcao), pick the specific targets, type a title and message, and send. The system resolves each target to a deduplicated list of users, delivers via SignalR to whoever is online right now, and persists the rest so offline users see them on next login. Both Blazor (INotificationService) and REST (POST /api/notification/send-to-department) entry points exist.

3How it works

When a user connects, the system auto-joins their connection to several SignalR groups: their personal User_* group, the global All group, their Company_* group, their Dept_*_* group, every Job_*_* group they belong to, and their Role_*_* group. To deliver a message, the sender names a target type and ID; the system queries the company DB for the matching employees, then queries the central DB to find which users those employees correspond to, dedupes, and pushes via the relevant group. Online users receive instantly via the existing connection; offline users get a database row that surfaces on next login - the 'voicemail' fallback.

How Messages Find People

The Address Resolution System

Think of it like sending mail to "IT Department, Building A":

  1. You say: "Send to IT Department"
  2. System looks up: Who works in IT Department?
  3. System finds: Maria, Joao, Pedro (employees)
  4. System checks: Which employees have app accounts?
  5. System delivers: Notification to Maria, Joao, Pedro's accounts

SignalR Groups Explained Simply

The Walkie-Talkie Channels

Imagine everyone has a walkie-talkie with multiple channels:

Channel Who Listens
Channel "All" Everyone in the building
Channel "Admins" Only administrators
Channel "Company_1" Only people in Company 1
Channel "Dept_1_3" IT Department in Company 1
Channel "Job_1_42" Project X team in Company 1

When you join a channel, you hear all messages on that channel.

Auto-Joining Channels

When you "turn on your walkie-talkie" (connect to the app):

        ▼
System automatically puts you on:
  ✓ Your personal channel (User_123)
  ✓ "Everyone" channel (All)
  ✓ Your company channel (Company_1)
  ✓ Your department channel (Dept_1_3)
  ✓ Your project channels (Job_1_42)
  ✓ Your role channel (Role_1_7)

Common Questions

Q: Why can't I see any departments?

A: You need to select a company first. Departments belong to companies.

Q: What's the difference between Grupos and Departamentos?

A:

  • Grupos = System user groups (like "Admins", "Managers")
  • Departamentos = Real company departments (like "IT", "HR", "Finance")

Q: Will offline users get my notification?

A: Yes! It's saved to the database and they'll see it when they log in.

Q: Can I send to multiple target types at once?

A: Yes! Select from different tabs - they'll all receive the message.

Key takeaways

  • SignalR groups are addressable channels - join one and you hear everything broadcast on it
  • Multi-target notification works by resolving each target tab into a deduplicated user list before broadcasting
  • Offline does not mean lost: notifications persist to the DB and surface on next login
  • The same notification engine should expose both an in-process service (for Blazor) and a REST controller (for mobile/integrations)
  • Auto-joining a user to multiple groups on connect is what makes targeting cheap at send time
